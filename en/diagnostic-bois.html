Holes in the wood, sawdust, crumbling timber: identify the insect and treat it

Match what you see to the insect responsible

  • Round holes of 1 to 3 mm + fine, light-coloured sawdust: woodworm (common furniture beetle)
  • Oval holes of 6 to 10 mm + gnawing sounds at night: house longhorn beetle
  • Layered wood that sounds hollow, mud tubes, no sawdust at all: termites
  • Rough shavings + large black ants in the evening: carpenter ants
  • Very fine, flour-like dust on light-coloured wood: powderpost beetle (lyctus)
  • Brownish wood breaking into cubes, mushroom smell: dry rot or another wood-decay fungus

Insects in the wood: how serious is it?

Holes, sawdust or crumbling timber mean larvae have been tunnelling through your wood for months, often years. As long as the infestation is active, the damage keeps spreading in silence: a floorboard or a piece of furniture can be replaced, but a damaged roof structure can cost tens of thousands of euros if nothing is done. The only way to know how serious it is, is to have it checked: recent activity shows up when the timber is probed and when the frass is examined for freshness.

From diagnosis to roof timber treatment by injection

If the assessment confirms an active infestation, we follow up with the appropriate treatment. For a roof structure, the professional protocol is well proven: the worm-eaten parts are cut back to sound wood, injection wells are drilled into the beams, purlins and rafters, then a certified product is injected under pressure into the heart of the timber. This treatment of structural timber also applies to individual beams, floors and timber frames. On lightly affected wood, a surface spray completes the injection. For termites, the treatment combines injection, a chemical barrier or bait stations depending on the configuration; for carpenter ants, destroying the nest is systematic, otherwise the colony re-forms.

Frequently asked questions

I found sawdust at the base of a beam, is it serious?

Fresh sawdust almost always means wood-boring insects are active inside the timber. The species and the severity depend on the shape of the sawdust and the holes: fine dust and round holes point to woodworm, coarse frass and oval holes to the house longhorn beetle, rough shavings to carpenter ants. The right reflex is to have it checked quickly.

How can I tell whether holes in the wood are old or active?

Holes with light-coloured edges accompanied by fresh sawdust indicate an active infestation. Dark holes with no sawdust are often old, but larvae may still be working below the surface. Probing the timber during the technical visit settles the question.

What is worm-eaten wood and can it be saved?

Worm-eaten wood is timber whose core has been hollowed out by larval galleries: it crumbles, sounds hollow and loses its strength. In most cases it can be saved: the worst-affected parts are cut back to sound wood, then the timber is treated by deep injection. Replacement is only required when the piece has lost its load-bearing capacity.
